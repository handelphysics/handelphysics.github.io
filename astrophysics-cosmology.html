Astrophysics & Cosmology

In the standard ΛCDM picture of cosmology, about 27% of the universe is dark matter and about 68% is dark energy, and neither has been identified. Searches for dark-matter particles such as WIMPs, axions and sterile neutrinos have so far come up empty. Handel’s recent work proposes that neither requires new particles or new physics: both follow from Einstein’s general relativity once the gravitational field of the universe itself is taken into account.

In this picture, dark matter is the energy of the coherent gravitational field of the universe, a field that is in large part its own source. This field energy is drawn into galaxies and clusters in proportion to their own gravitational field, following a single universal density formula. From it come the flat rotation curves of stars in galaxies and the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation, without adjustable dark-matter halos. The follow-up work extends the same space-time approach to dark energy and the cosmological constant.

Key Publications

#317
Calculation of Dark Matter as a Feature of Space–Time: Two Geometries, Derivation of Rotation Curves, Universal Dark Matter Density Formula and Tully–Fisher Relations (2023)
P.H. Handel, K.E. Splett
Foundations of Physics 53, 86 (2023) · Open Access · doi:10.1007/s10701-023-00705-x
Derives the first analytical formula for the density of dark matter at all length scales, starting from Einstein’s equations and their Newtonian approximation. Part I defines dark matter as the energy of the coherent gravitational field of the universe, the extra mass-equivalent needed to explain the observed gravitational field, and shows this field is largely its own source. Part II describes how this dark matter, created at the scale of the universe, is drawn into galaxies and clusters in proportion to their own gravitational field g, through the universal formula ρD = λgg′², where g′ is the field of the universe and λ is a universal constant fixed by the Tully–Fisher relations. The result reproduces galactic rotation curves and the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation in agreement with observations. The paper opens with an electromagnetic analogy to keep the argument accessible.

#400
Derivation of Dark Matter, Dark Energy and the Cosmological Constant as Features of Space-Time (2024)
P.H. Handel, K.E. Splett
Manuscript, November 2024, with Supplement 1: The Nature of Dark Energy
Proposes a complete solution of the dark matter and dark energy problems from general relativity alone. Dark matter is identified as the energy of the coherent gravitational field of the observable universe. By anticipating the solution of Einstein’s equations for the observable universe, the paper derives simple universal formulas for dark matter at all scales, the dark energy density and the cosmological constant. Its “DM key”, ρD = Qg with Q = 1.39·10⁻¹⁶ g s²/cm⁴, gives the dark matter accreted per unit of local Newtonian gravitational field. From it the paper derives galactic rotation curves, extended halos, the Tully–Fisher relations and a generalized MOND, and obtains Λ = 1.08·10⁻⁵⁶ cm⁻² and ρΛ = 5.8·10⁻³⁰ g/cm³, within 3% of observation. Supplement 1 discusses the nature of dark energy and dark matter.
